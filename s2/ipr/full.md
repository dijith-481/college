**Module 2**
**Problem and Solution Canvas Preparation**

**Problem and solution canvas preparation**
*   Be open to change.
*   Consider the customer.
*   Ask questions.
*   Don't assume a solution.

**01. CUSTOMER STATE FIT**
*   **Customer state:**
    *   Who is your customer?
    *   What limits customers to act when problem occurs?
    *   Which solutions are available?
    *   What had he/she tried in the past?
    *   What are the pros/cons of existing solutions?

**02. PROBLEM-BEHAVIOR FIT**
*   **Problem and related behavior:**
    *   Which problem do you solve?
    *   What is the cause of every problem?
    *   What does your customer do about the problem?
    *   How often does this related behavior happen?

**03. COMMUNICATION-CHANNEL FIT**
*   **Triggers, emotions and channels:**
    *   What triggers customer to act?
    *   Which emotions do people feel before/after problem resolved?
    *   Where online does this behavior happen?

**04. PROBLEM-SOLUTION FIT**
*   **Your solution:**
    *   Fill in after overview of real situation (new solution).
    *   Fill in first (existing solution).
    *   Spot patterns, repeated keywords by listing problems/behavior.
    *   Think of possible solutions fitting user state limitations.
    *   Take best from alternative solutions.
    *   Resemble natural triggers.
    *   Tap into existing customer behavior.
    *   Design useful, understandable, accessible solution.

**3) Customer profiling**
*   **Definition:** Analyzing customer data to create detailed picture of ideal customer.
*   **Can help businesses:**
    *   Understand their customers.
    *   Personalize their marketing.
    *   Improve customer service.
    *   Develop tailored strategies.

**B2B vs. B2C customer profiles:**
*   **B2B customer profiles:** company size, industry, location, revenue, target audience, decision-makers.
*   **B2C customer profiles:** individual customers, demographic data (age, gender, lifestyle preferences).

**Benefits of customer profiling (Visual elements):**
*   Improves efficiency.
*   Drives loyalty.
*   Increases cross-team collaboration.
*   Boosts sales.
*   Gathers insights.

**Types of customer profiling:**
*   **Demographic profiling:** Defines customers by personal characteristics.
    *   Age.
    *   Gender.
    *   Marital status.
    *   Ethnicity.
    *   Income.
    *   Job title.
    *   Education.
*   **Psychographic profiling:** Defines why customers buy.
    *   Personality traits.
    *   Attitudes.
    *   Opinions.
    *   Values and beliefs.
    *   Lifestyle.
    *   Religions.
    *   Political affiliation.
*   **Consumer typology method:** Analyzes by motivation, mindset, engagement.
    *   Loyal consumers.
    *   Discount consumers.
    *   Impulse consumers.
    *   Need-based consumers.
*   **Consumer characteristics strategy:** Looks at traits influencing buying decisions.
    *   Convenience-driven.
    *   Connectivity-driven.
    *   Personalization-driven.
*   **Behavioral profiling:** Defines how customers interact with your brand.
    *   Buying patterns.
    *   Spending habits.
    *   Brand interactions.
    *   How they use products/services.
    *   Types of feedback.
*   **Geographic profiling:** Defines customers by physical location.
    *   Physical location.
    *   Language.
    *   Culture.
    *   Workplace.

**How to create customer profiles:**
1.  Determine problem product/service solves.
2.  Gather customer information (journey map, demographics).
3.  Collect customer feedback (face-to-face, video/phone interviews).
4.  Understand customer habits (loyalty programs, surveys, inventory, targeted sales).
5.  Look at the competition.
6.  Create a customer profile (product/service fit, demographics, benefits, pain points, social media habits).
7.  Keep customer profile up to date.

**6) Financial projections**
*   **Definition:** Internal process to explain recent earnings/growth.
*   Extrapolates data to anticipate future.

**Three primary financial statements:**
1.  Income statement.
2.  Cash flow projection.
3.  Balance sheet.

**Income Statement (P&L):**
*   Shows profitability over time.
*   **Key sections:** Revenue, Expenses, Total income, Net income.
*   **Critical ratios:** Profitability, Efficiency, Liquidity, Leverage.

**Balance Sheet:**
*   Snapshot of net worth at a moment in time.
*   Summarizes financial data in: Assets, Liabilities, Equity.
*   Assets = Liabilities + Equity (must be balanced).

**Cash Flow Protection:**
*   Shows money flowing in/out.
*   Used by lenders/investors for credit risk.
*   **Key sections:** Cash revenues, Cash disbursements, Reconciliation.
*   **Financial forecasting helps:** Forge clear path, Build investor confidence, Identify resource allocation.

**7) Risk management**
*   **Definition:** Identifying, analyzing, prioritizing risks; taking action to reduce impact.
*   Helps organizations prepare for threats, protect reputation/profitability/sustainability.

**Risk management process steps:**
1.  Identify risks (Property, Liability, Business income, HR, Crime, Employee benefit, Foreign, Intangible, Non-compliance).
    *   **Sources:** Questionnaires, checklists, physical inspection, flowcharts, financial statements, historical loss data.
2.  Analyze risks (likelihood, impact).
3.  Prioritize risks (based on impact).
4.  Take action (mitigate, control negative outcomes).
5.  Monitor and report.

**Objectives:**
*   **Pre-Loss Objectives:** Economy, Reduction of Anxiety, Meeting Legal Obligations.
*   **Post-Loss Objectives:** Survival, Continued Operating, Stability of Earnings, Continued Growth, Social Responsibility.

**Risk Control Techniques:**
*   Avoidance.
*   Loss Prevention.
*   Loss Reduction.
*   Duplication.
*   Separation.
*   Diversification.

**Prototype development plan preparation**

**1) Prototype requirements analysis**
*   Prototyping: Developing working replication of product/system.
*   Requirements analysis: Crucial for defining prototype.

**Steps of Prototyping Model:**
*   Step 1: Requirement Gathering and Analysis.
*   Step 2: Quick Design.
*   Step 3: Build a Prototype.
*   Step 4: Initial User Evaluation.
*   Step 5: Refining Prototype.
*   Step 6: Implement Product and Maintain.

**Types of Prototyping Models:**
*   Rapid Throwaway Prototyping.
*   Evolutionary Prototyping.
*   Incremental Prototyping.
*   Extreme Prototyping.

**Rapid Throwaway Prototyping:**
*   Explores ideas, gets customer feedback.
*   Prototype not necessarily part of accepted final.
*   Customer feedback prevents design faults.

**Evolutionary Prototyping:**
*   Initial prototype incrementally refined.
*   Saves time and effort vs. throwing away.

**Incremental Prototyping:**
*   Final product broken into small prototypes, developed individually.
*   Merged into single final product.
*   Reduces complexity, speeds up delivery.

**Extreme Prototyping (for web dev):**
*   Phase 1: Basic prototype (HTML static pages).
*   Phase 2: Functional screens (simulated data).
*   Phase 3: Services implemented, associated with prototype.

**Advantages of Prototyping Model:**
*   Customers see partial product early (satisfaction).
*   New requirements easily accommodated (refinement scope).
*   Missing functionalities easily figured out.
*   Errors detected earlier (saves cost/effort, enhances quality).
*   Developed prototype reusable.
*   Flexibility in design.
*   Early feedback.
*   Tests/validates design decisions.
*   Reduces project failure risk.
*   Facilitates communication/collaboration.
*   Bridges gap between technical/non-technical stakeholders.

**Disadvantages of the Prototyping Model:**
*   Costly (time/money).
*   Too much variation in requirements.
*   Poor Documentation.
*   Difficult to accommodate all changes.
*   Uncertainty in number of iterations.
*   Customers may demand actual product too soon.
*   Developers may build sub-optimal solutions.
*   Customer might lose interest.
*   Prototype may not be scalable.
*   Prototype may not accurately represent final product.
*   Focus may shift from final product.
*   Prototype may give false sense of completion.
*   Prototype may not consider technical feasibility/scalability.
*   Prototype may be developed using different tools/technologies (training/maintenance costs).
*   Prototype may not reflect actual business requirements.

**Applications of Prototyping Model:**
*   Requirements not clearly understood/unstable.
*   Requirements changing quickly.
*   Developing user interfaces, high-technology/software-intensive systems, complex algorithms/interfaces.
*   Demonstrates technical feasibility.

**6) Testing and quality assurance**
*   Software Testing (ST) and Quality Assurance (QA) are distinct processes.
*   **ST:** Identify/verify software meets requirements, find defects.
*   **QA:** Ensure product meets specified quality standards (continuous process).

**Software Testing:**
*   Executing program to find defects/errors.
*   Verifies functionality, meets requirements/design.
*   Includes unit, functional, integration, system, regression testing.
*   Primary focus: Identify/fix defects before release.

**Characteristics of Software Testing:**
*   Purpose: Identify defects/gaps.
*   Scope: Functionality, uncovering defects.
*   Involvement: Testers actively involved.
*   Techniques: Automation tools, manual tests.
*   Responsibilities: Ensure quality standards met.
*   Deliverables: Report (defects list, summary, recommendations).

**Advantages of Software Testing:**
*   Enhances Quality.
*   Early Detection.
*   Cost Savings.
*   Improved User Experience.

**Limitations of Software Testing:**
*   Time-consuming.
*   Limited in scope.
*   Based on assumptions.
*   Complex and time-consuming.

**Applications of Software Testing:**
*   Unit Testing.
*   Integration Testing.
*   System Testing.
*   Acceptance Testing.
*   Performance Testing.
*   Security Testing.

**Quality Assurance:**
*   Ensuring product meets specified quality standards.
*   Continuous process (planning, designing, developing, testing).
*   Focuses on overall process and best practices.

**Characteristics of Quality Assurance:**
*   Purpose: Ensure all requirements met, no defects.
*   Scope: Implementation of processes/procedures/standards.
*   Involvement: QA professionals monitor development, review documentation, audit.
*   Techniques: Automation tools, manual inspections.
*   Responsibilities: Ensure compliance with industry standards.
*   Deliverables: Report (non-conformances, audit summary, recommendations).

**Advantages of Quality Assurance:**
*   Improves Reliability.
*   Increases Customer Satisfaction.
*   Enhances Usability.
*   Increased Productivity.
*   Improved Customer Satisfaction.

**Limitations of Quality Assurance:**
*   Cost (personnel, tools).
*   Human Error.
*   Process Limitation.
*   Knowledge Limitation.

**Applications of Quality Assurance:**
*   Design and Code Reviews.
*   Process Auditing.
*   Automated Testing.
*   Defect Tracking.
*   Configuration Management.
*   Risk Management.

**Differences between Software Testing and Quality Assurance (Table summary):**
*   **Responsibilities:** ST (finding/documenting defects), QA (ensuring product meets requirements).
*   **Techniques:** ST (unit, functional, integration, system testing), QA (root cause analysis, process audits, defect management).
*   **Output:** ST (list of defects), QA (assurance product meets requirements).
*   **Cost:** ST (generally costs more), QA (generally costs less).
*   **Time:** ST (generally takes more time), QA (generally takes less time).
*   **Effort:** ST (requires more effort), QA (requires less effort).
*   **Testing:** ST (process of executing program), QA (not a testing process).
*   **Risk:** ST (risk with finding/documenting defects), QA (risk with ensuring requirements met).
*   **Documentation:** ST (documenting defects), QA (documenting process to ensure requirements met).
*   **Cost Effectiveness:** ST (not always cost-effective), QA (usually cost-effective).

**7) Iterative development and feedback loop**
*   Important parts of Agile methodologies and software development.

**Iterative development:**
*   Breaks process into smaller chunks (iterations/sprints).
*   Each iteration includes planning, design, development, testing.

**Feedback loop:**
*   Output used as input for future operations.
*   Allows system to self-adjust and evolve.

**Allow teams to:**
*   Continuously improve.
*   Adapt to changes.
*   Enhance teamwork.
*   Identify areas for improvement.
*   Adjust strategies.

**Positive Feedback Loops:**
*   Output amplifies system behavior.
*   Result feeds back into system to repeat process.

**Negative Feedback Loops:**
*   Output dampens system behavior.
*   Self-regulating mechanism for stability.

**Importance of Feedback Loops in Software Development:**
*   Integral to continuous learning and improvement.
*   Used in Agile methodologies.
